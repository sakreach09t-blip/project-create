The person behind the pixels.

A closer look at how I got into front-end work, what I believe about building interfaces, and a few of the people I've built them for.

How I got here
01 — Story
2019
First website, built out of stubbornness
Taught myself HTML and CSS by rebuilding a local coffee shop's menu page from scratch, just to prove I could match the fonts exactly. I couldn't, not for another year — but I kept trying.
2021
Went deep on interaction detail
Started obsessing over the small stuff — easing curves, focus states, the exact blur radius that makes a glass panel feel like glass instead of fog. Built dozens of login and registration forms just to practice depth and motion.
2023
First freelance clients
Took on my first paid front-end projects, mostly branded e-commerce sites, and learned that a design only matters if it still looks right on a cracked phone screen in bad wifi.
Now
Building from Phnom Penh, working everywhere
Splitting time between client work and personal practice — still rebuilding interfaces I admire, still chasing the 2px that's slightly off.
How I work
02 — Principles
01

Detail is the design

Spacing, easing, and focus states aren't polish added at the end — they're decided alongside the layout, from the first draft.

02

Understand every effect

If I can't explain why a shadow or blur looks right, I don't ship it. Plain HTML and CSS keep every decision debuggable.

03

Build for the real device

A layout isn't finished until it holds up on a small screen, a slow connection, and a keyboard-only pass.
